Hedgerow

Tuesday 11 July 1944, Normandy. Your American rifle company must fight through the bocage, field by field, and take a village three fields in from the German end of the board (Saint-Jean or La Haye: it changes each game), then hold it at the end of a round, or destroy every German on the field. The game is played in rounds; there is no limit for now. The moment one side has no pieces left, the game is over (the round isn't finished); if one mission or bomb takes the last of both sides, it is a draw. You lose at the end of a round if you have no rifle platoon left and nothing left that can hurt the German tanks. A German garrison holds the village; more Germans march up from the east.

  • 🪖 Pieces: your company: three rifle platoons (three sections of three men each, moving together), the captain, machine-gun and bazooka teams, a sniper, and two tanks: a Sherman and a Firefly. 1st Platoon is elite, and so is the Germans' 1. Fallschirmjäger-Zug: an elite piece has a gold band round the edge of each of its bases, its men look the part (and a ★ after its name): 1st Platoon are Rangers in brown, with the orange diamond on their helmets; the Fallschirmjäger wear the paratrooper's camouflage smock and black helmet. The other Germans are in field grey. Elite troops need one less to hit, in shooting and hand to hand. Your bases are green, the Germans' brown. The Germans: Fallschirmjäger platoons, MG 42 and Panzerschreck teams, a PaK 40 gun with its crew of three (it turns to face whatever it fires at), a Panzer IV and a Panther in panzer grey (the Panther's armour needs one more to hit, by shot or shell; every tank's armour shows in its panel and on the red label), and their captain with a red flag, who rallies their shaken pieces. Both captains can be shot and shelled like anyone else (a small target): one wound and he's gone.
  • 🗺️ Maps: the same start also asks which map, or Random. Each has its own ground, and most change the pieces, the guns or where the sides start: River crossing (bridges the only way over), Crossroads town, Open farmland (few hedges, barns and a duck pond), Paris (a new maze of cobbled streets each game) and Deep bocage (hedges nearly everywhere). A café or a barn takes three areas along one side of its field and blocks the view as a tank does; a fountain or a pond can't be stood on, but can be seen over. Some give a side two bomber runs. The choice says what each one changes.
  • 🔄 Human or robot: the game starts by asking who commands each side, and New game asks again. Two humans share the screen and take turns token by token: a piece under fire is asked Stand fast or Take cover by the other player. Two robots play it out while you watch, a draw after 30 rounds. Playing the Germans: As the Germans you win only by destroying every American piece on the table; their mortars, artillery and bomber off the table don't count. You lose if a rifle platoon of theirs takes the village and holds it at the end of a round, with no Germans left in it, or if every German is destroyed. The computer plays the Americans and attacks. Everything else in these rules works the same, the other way round: your pieces fight with German weapons and your mortars drop on one piece, not a whole field.
  • 📋 The bag: each round a token goes in a blind bag for every piece on the table: green with a gold star for yours, grey with a black cross for the Germans', and one black timer token (a white sand-glass): when the timer is drawn the round ends there and then, whoever has still to move (it is never the first token out). Tokens are drawn one at a time; on yours, pick one piece and give it its move (or Skip turn in its menu); on theirs, a German piece moves. A piece destroyed, or one that loses its move, takes a token out. When the bag is empty the next round starts. The tokens left are shown in the bar at the bottom (the timer between the two sides'); the one drawn flies onto the table above that side's artillery and stays there until its move is over. Your mortars, artillery and bomber each have a token too, while they have rounds or a run left (with none left, they come out of the bag): on one of your tokens, firing them or flying the bomber uses it. Any piece or gun can Skip turn (in its menu): it gives up its move this round and the token is used.
  • 🔍 Looking at a German piece: click it to ring it and see its card in the bottom panel. Every card has a picture of the piece.
  • 🖱️ Picking a piece shows where it can go straight away (picking the captain also puts a blue target on every piece he can rally, himself too: click one to rally it; with nobody to rally there is no Rally; after he moves or fires, his menu opens again with Rally if he can still rally someone): red-hatched fields are a Move & shoot (it moves there, then fires), the other marked fields a Charge. Click the field (where in it you want to stand) and that's the action. The menu by the piece has the rest.
  • 🎲 Actions (click a piece for its menu): Charge: up to three fields (bazooka and Panzerschreck teams four), without shooting, and through a field of your own pieces on the way, but not through a village or hamlet (only rifle platoons fight hand to hand, if Germans hold the field it ends in; a tank can't fight hand to hand: it drives in on men and they fall back to a field away from it, or are overrun if they have nowhere to go), Move & shoot (up to two fields, bazooka and Panzerschreck teams three, to a red-hatched field from where it will have someone to fire at, then fire needing one more; machine guns can't fire on the move: they Charge to a new spot, then fire), Shoot (full dice), and the captain's Rally: not his move, he rallies twice a round if he stays put or charges, in any order (two then the charge, one either side of it, or the charge then two), once if he shoots or moves and shoots (before or after it), himself included. After two rallies only a charge is left. To stop before using them all, pick Skip turn in his menu. He can Charge, Move & shoot and Shoot too, with his submachine gun: three dice, at close range only (four areas away or nearer); he can't shoot further than that. There is no plain move: to move without shooting, Charge.
  • 🪤 Ambush: in its menu, beside Skip turn, any piece can lie in ambush instead of moving (a dashed amber ring pulses under it). It fires as soon as an enemy moves or shoots in its sight and can be hit on 6 or less, anywhere along the enemy's move. Pieces already in sight when it goes in aren't fired on until they move or shoot. If the enemy isn't killed it carries on, unless the shot shakes it: then it stops there (it can still fire if it was on a Move & shoot and has someone to fire at; a charge ends there). An ambush fires once, and ends with the round. Firing gives it away: an enemy ambush that can see it fires on it in turn.
  • 👁️ Who can shoot whom: along a line of sight over open fields and lanes. Seeing has no limit: any piece (moved this round or not) sees the whole length of the board where nothing blocks the view, and that is enough to call the mortars or artillery onto a field; shooting with its own weapon reaches only so far (below). Villages, hamlets and orchards can't be seen through, but a piece in one can see out and be seen, wherever it stands in it (they are all cover, below). A line just touching the corner of one gets by only for a piece in a corner area at that very point (both +1), as at a hedge end. From outside, a piece sees into a village, hamlet or orchard field at any angle or distance, along any straight line that reaches it (those inside keep their cover, and see out the same way); it never sees through a field like that, so one next to it hides what lies behind it. Sight runs in straight lines between the middles of areas (each field is 3×3 areas). Each hedge is three thirds, one against each edge area beside it: a third blocks a line that crosses it unless the shooter or the target stands in an area touching that third (a target touching it is in cover, +1). Two fields corner to corner see each other along the 45° line through their shared corner (villages, hamlets and orchards too), and the line runs on corner to corner to the edge of the board; but past a hedge ending at such a corner only a piece standing in a corner area at that very point sees, and then the hedge gives both +1 cover, unless either of them touches that hedge at the corner. A big piece sees and is seen from whichever of its areas does best. How far: rifles 4 fields (long range beyond 2), machine guns and the PaK 6 (long range beyond 4), tanks 7 (long range beyond 4), bazookas and Panzerschrecks 4 (long range beyond 1); one more is needed on the dice at long range. The sniper sees as far as the board goes, and neither cover nor range helps his target. A tank blocks every line of sight through the areas it stands in (both sides); past a tank's corner only a piece in an area touching that corner sees, along the 45° line, and both get +1 cover unless either is right beside that side of the tank. Red targets show who a piece can hit, with the score its dice need.
  • 🌳 Lining a hedge: a piece stands at one of nine places in its field: a corner, the middle of an edge, or the centre. Where you click in a field is where it goes, if no other piece is there (the nearest free place otherwise). Against an edge it lines that hedge; in a corner it lines both; in the centre it is in the open. While you choose, a green bar shows the hedge it would line (cover). Across a hedge: a piece lining it can fire through; a piece in the open can only fire at an enemy who lines it, needing one more on the dice; two pieces in the open can't see each other; two both lining it fire at each other needing one more. To move to another place in its own field, Charge and click there in its own field (green): its move for the round.
  • 🧍 Sharing a field: every field is nine areas, three by three: the middle, four edges and four corners. Most pieces take one area; the PaK 40 two side by side (it can't leave its field, or in the large village of four fields it can go anywhere in that village, but on its token it can move to another place, facing out of the area you click, one of eight ways (to the middle, the way it came from): that is its move, and it can't fire after, though it can lie in ambush on the same go); a tank, or a rifle platoon of two or three sections, four in a block: a corner, the two edges beside it and the middle; or it stands in the middle of the field, keeping the four edges clear round it (only the corners left for others) and touching no hedge, so it is hidden from pieces across the hedges unless they touch them (a platoon down to one section takes one area). Pieces share a field as long as their areas don't overlap, so one big piece a field. A piece in any of the eight areas round the edge lines the hedges it is against (a big piece in a corner block lines the two of its corner). Click the area to go to; a piece can also move to another area of its own field: that is its move, and it may fire after, one more needed for moving (not a machine gun, nor a captain with no shot left). Only hand-to-hand fighting brings both sides into the same field.
  • 🏘️ Villages and hamlets: small houses along every side of the field, pieces in the yard between them. Houses are hard cover: two more needed to hit anyone there. Only a rifle platoon takes the village: when one can reach it, the village field shows blue hatching.
  • ✈️ The bomber: each side has one, a B-17 or a Ju 88, parked below its mortars with its own token, and one run a game (two on some maps). Pick it and a compass spins above the board: that is the way it flies this round (pick it again and it spins to the same direction). Click any field round the edge of the board: it flies in from off the table on a straight line through that field and off the other side. Every field it passes over with a piece in it gets a bomb on each piece, yours too: one die, 5 or more (cover, armour and taking cover add as they do against shells), and a hit does what a mortar's does. Your pieces under a German bomber's line can take cover first. Skip turn keeps the run for another round.
  • 💣 Fire support: your 81 mm mortars and 105 mm artillery stand off the board behind your back line (left), the Germans' behind theirs (right). Click your mortars or artillery on the table to use them, like any other piece: on one of your tokens, Fire (or Skip turn). Their rounds fly from there, and the pile of rounds beside each shows how many shots it has left. Artillery also falls on the fields round the target, on your own pieces too (the target warns you). A tank takes one die from either: from artillery it counts as any other hit; from mortars it only shakes the tank, or knocks it out if it is already shaken. The Germans have mortars and artillery too, and their rounds are piled the same way. Rounds never come back: once a gun has fired its last, it is out of the game for good.
  • 🔊 Sound: bombers droning over now and then, rifles, the American .50 cal and the German MG 42, bazookas, tank guns, mortar bombs and shells whistling in, men running, tanks driving, and a battle going on in the distance. The 🔊 button in the bar at the bottom turns it all off.
  • ✂️ Barbed wire closes some gaps between fields: only tanks get through it, rolling over and flattening it, and one time in three the tank gets snagged and stops in that field (the wire is flattened either way). Men must go round, or wait for a tank to flatten it.
  • 🌳 Hedges: none grow round villages, hamlets or orchards. men climb over a hedge bank as they move (they hop up over it on the table), which uses one more move (the hedge stays); tanks drive straight through, leaving a gap. Charging across a hedge at Germans behind it is allowed, but they fight back hard.
  • ⚔️ Hand to hand: a charge into a field the enemy holds is a fight to the finish. The two sides strike in turn, the attacker first: every base still standing rolls a die (3+, the attacker one more against men behind the hedge it crossed or in houses; elite one less), and each hit takes a base off the other side. The survivors fight on until only one side is left; the attacker takes the field if it wins. Every piece in the defenders' field fights, even one that has moved or is taking cover. Being shaken doesn't count in the fight, and survivors who were shaken are still shaken after it. On the table each blow is shown in turn: the striking side lunges, Hit! or Miss rises for each die, and each base lost drops away.
  • 🛡️ Tanks go one field further on a Charge or a Move & shoot if they start, stay and end on road or lane, or a mix of both, not when shaken. They can't be hurt by rifles or machine guns: only bazookas, Panzerschrecks, anti-tank guns, other tanks and the artillery can knock them out, and infantry can't charge a field held only by tanks. A tank firing on men hits every piece on foot in that field, like a mortar.
  • 🔥 Wrecks: a knocked-out tank burns and stays where it died, black and scorched. It blocks the view through the three squares it takes (a corner and the two edges beside it), and past its corner a piece touching that corner sees along the 45° line, both +1, as with a live tank. The middle stays free, so a full platoon or a tank can still come in at the opposite corner. A second wreck in a field clears the older one away.
  • 🎲 Dice: every shot rolls six-sided dice by the target: 3 or more hits. One more is needed for each of: a target in cover (lining a hedge; in an orchard, or a tank in a village or hamlet; two more for men in a village or hamlet: houses are hard cover; against mortars and artillery any of these is just one more; and none at all from a village, hamlet or orchard when the shooter is in the very same field), a small target (two men or fewer: the sniper, a bazooka team, the captain; not an MG team, which is three), long range, firing on the move, and the shooter being shaken. A shot that would need 7 or more is a Hail Mary: any die that throws a 6 throws again, and only a second 6 hits (about 3%). It says HAIL MARY on the red label, for shots, mortars and artillery alike. Elite troops need one less; the Panther's armour one more. Close range: one less for every shot at a target four areas away or nearer, in a straight line between the nearest areas of the two pieces (a field is three areas across, so about a field and a third); not mortars, artillery or bombs. A 1 always misses, however good the shot. On a Move & shoot, crossed red hatching marks the areas where it would have a shot at close range. Bazookas, Panzerschrecks, the PaK and tank guns kill men with every hit. Every hit shakes the target, and a coin toss says whether it wounds as well (a section, a team or a tank lost). A hit on a piece already shaken kills. The dice of every attack (shots, shells and hand to hand, both sides) land in the top corner of the table above the attacking side's artillery once the attack has struck, the newest at the bottom and up to three a side, each with a tag saying who rolled, the score needed and the hits: hits glow yellow, misses are dimmed.
  • 🟢 Who can move: a green ring under a piece means it can still move this round; an American piece that has finished its move turns light green, a German one grey.
  • 😵 Shaken (a red ! token on the edge of its base, or on a tank's back deck): a piece that is hit is shaken. It can still act, but it needs one more to hit, and it moves and charges only half as far (at least one field). Only a rally clears it, and a rally always works: the piece can use its move to rally itself, or the captain can rally it, up to three fields away. It stays shaken through the end of the round and through a fight. A hit on a piece already shaken kills: a section, a team or a tank lost.
  • 🛡️ Taking cover: when one of your pieces (not a tank) is fired on, a word bubble by it asks: Stand fast or Take cover. Taking cover: its men get down where they are (they crouch on the table), and every shot or shell at it needs two more for the rest of the round ("+2 take cover" on the red label; its panel says +2 cover; the sniper ignores it). The shot still happens; if that puts the score at 7 or more it is a Hail Mary. It uses up its move this round, and the cover wears off next round; the captain can get it up sooner (Rally, up to three fields away). A captain in cover can't rally anyone, himself included, until it wears off. A piece that has already moved this round can't take cover. Every piece of yours that German artillery or a German bomber will fall on is asked in turn, before they land. A shaken German rallies itself the same way, unless it has a shot and its captain is near. The Germans take cover too, when it is wise: a shaken piece as soon as a hit is likely, a weakened one against a fair volley, a whole one only against a heavy one (shots and shells alike).
  • 🧩 The table: 28 mm-style models: three men to a round section base, three sections to a platoon; one tank to a tank unit. The men are bigger models (1:54) than the tanks (1:76), and the houses small, about as tall as a figure.

Click one of your pieces: where it can go shows at once, and its menu opens; click a field, or pick from the menu. Esc cancels.
