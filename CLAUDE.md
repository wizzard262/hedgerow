# Hedgerow

Local repo `C:\DEV\Repositories\GitHub\hedgerow`, on GitHub as wizzard262/hedgerow with Pages at wizzard262.github.io/hedgerow (ask Steve before every push). A browser game: July 1944, an American rifle company fights through the Normandy bocage to take a village (played in rounds, no hours; no limit for now; TURNS in data.js sets one). Shown as a wargame table in 3D (three.js): flocked mat, foam hedges, villages (the river is off the map for now, Steve 2026-10-31; its code stays), painted miniatures and model tanks, dice for every shot.

- After every prompt about the game, add a short summary of it to [PROMPTS.md](PROMPTS.md), with any assumptions made
- At the end of each session, record its length in the **Sessions** table at the bottom of PROMPTS.md (keep that table last)
- After each change to the game, save a numbered screenshot in `screenshots/` (`06-short-name.png`, …), taken in a visible browser, then add it to `screenshots/list.json`
  - Serve the game with `python -m http.server 8768` in this folder, then `node tools/shot.js <name> [hours to play]`. It borrows Playwright from `..\harrier-carrier-playwright-tests`
- Run `node tools/test.js` before every commit (headless checks of the rules). After changing `ui.js`, `main.js` or `board3d.js`, also run `node tools/check-page.js` (real clicks in a visible browser)
- After a rules change, run `node tools/sweep.js ""` (150 games with a simple attacking bot, token by token as the page plays; about 4 minutes): with no time limit it plays up to 20 rounds: note its win rate and average rounds, both ways round (as the Americans, and as the Germans against the American computer); doing nothing must never win
- Bonus battles (Steve, 2026-11-11): `BONUS` in `js/data.js`, outside `CAMPAIGN`; a map with `bonus: true` is in MAPS but not the campaign. Dunkirk is the first: its `evacuate` settings, `names` (the 'us' side is the British), `guns` (renamed guns) and `us.only` / `de.only` (its own armies) are in its MAPS entry; the rules are under "The evacuation" in `js/sim.js` (boats, sailing, lorries, strongpoints, the British computer; the Germans win once one reaches the coast road). A map with `navy` has its artillery as a warship at sea, its name and shells off its bow (Dunkirk, the Normandy beach; no 'us' mortars and no headland on either, Steve 2026-11-12); on both the beach runs on past the top and bottom of the board (`addBeachBeyond` in board3d.js). Stalingrad is the second (Steve, 2026-11-12): `ruins` draws the hedges as ruined walls and rubble lines (`ruinWalls` in board3d.js) and the village as a ruined store; `generated: 'ruins'` (ruinsMap in sim.js) scatters rubble piles and minefields (FEATURES rubble, minefield; MINES); `army: 'su'` makes the 'us' side Soviet (Sim.armyOf, Models.setArmy, the red token); `flip` turns the camera right round (board3d's screenUp/screenRight; the off-board guns mirrored so they sit as usual on the screen). A rifle platoon with `arms: 'smg'` is a section of submachine gunners (SMG); `emplaced` in PIECES never moves (the 88, in the street beside the store, looking down it); `atGun` marks the PaK and the 88. A map's `guns` can rename its bomber too (`Sim.bomberName`: the RAF Blenheim at Dunkirk). Less than one die always rolls one (`dice` in sim.js, Steve 2026-11-12). New pieces can borrow a model with `look` in PIECES until they get their own
- Plain JavaScript, classic scripts loaded in order (no modules); three.js r128 from cdnjs
  - `js/data.js` the maps (MAPS: each can change the ground, the pieces, the ammunition and where the sides start) and every number to tune; `js/sim.js` the rules (no DOM); `js/sound.js` the battle sounds (Web Audio, no files); `js/models.js` the 3D models; `js/fire.js` how a burnt-out tank looks (flames, smoke, scorched hull); `js/chute.js` the shape and swing of a parachute coming down (no DOM, tested); `js/scenery.js` what is on the table just to look at (no DOM, no effect on the rules); `js/labels.js` where the red score bubbles go (no DOM); `js/board3d.js` the table, camera and picking; `js/ui.js` the left-hand panel; `js/main.js` start-up, input and the German hour
- The version number (`#ver` in `index.html`) goes up by 1 with every commit to the game: `git rev-list --count HEAD` + 1. Put the same number on every script link (`js/ui.js?v=2`)
- Each side can be a human or a robot (Steve, 2026-11-06): `st.mode` is 'us', 'de', 'both' or 'none', `Sim.isRobot(st, side)` says who plays a side, `st.human` is the side a person is commanding now (with two humans it changes token by token) and `st.computer` the computer's token in play. `germanAct` and `americanAct` in `js/sim.js` are the two computer players. `st.won` always means the Americans won and `st.lost` the Germans did; the page turns that into a win or a loss for the player
- Both sides behave identically (Steve, 2026-11-03): the same rules, moves, targeting and information on the table for the Germans as for the Americans; the only difference is that the Germans' pop-up menu (Skip turn and so on) isn't shown
- Scales on the table (Steve): men 1:54, tanks 1:76, houses 1:144; company level (Steve): each rifle piece is a platoon of three section bases (three men each, Steve 2026-11-03), moving together; one tank to a tank unit (Steve 2026-11-03). In the code a rifle platoon's kind is still 'company' and its sections are `platoons`; loose formations that shuffle when they move. Whole board on the table, one fixed slanted camera, no fog of war
